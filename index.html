About

I'm currently an MSc student in Cybersecurity at Aalborg University in Copenhagen, building on a BSc in Informatics from the Ionian University in Greece.

My bachelor's thesis looked at online privacy: how websites and third parties quietly follow people across the web, which tools and habits actually protect them, and why so few users act on what they know. The gap between knowing about a threat and doing something about it is what drew me into security.

During my degree I focused on the security side of computing, including cryptography, network and communications security, information systems security, and privacy-enhancing technologies. I also attended the IPICS intensive programme on information and communication security in Portugal.

I also maintain a personal cybersecurity homelab, where I implement and test security techniques in a controlled environment. I work in Python, SQL and C++, and hold the Microsoft Azure Fundamentals certification. My projects are on GitHub.

In my free time I read comics and follow the Marvel Cinematic Universe.

Featured research

BSc Thesis · 2025 Ionian University Grade 10/10

Automatic online tracking: technologies, protection methods and user awareness

A mixed-methods study combining a 15-year literature review of tracking techniques (cookies, fingerprinting, cross-site tracking) and their countermeasures (anti-tracking tools, VPNs, privacy browsers) with a survey of 68 Greek internet users. The findings point to a clear privacy paradox: people know tracking exists, but rarely act on it.

Know what web cookies are79.4%
Accept cookies without reading, for faster access85.3%
Feel they have low or very low control over their data76.5%
Never use anti-tracking tools64.7%
Can tell first-party from third-party cookies17.6%

Takeaway → Simpler policies, user education and transparent practices are needed so people can make informed choices about their data.
